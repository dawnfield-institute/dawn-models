# Round 8: the MoE continuous learner — it works, and the freeze-the-trunk intuition inverts

**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Runs:** frozen_ref / experts / full — OLMoE-1B-7B-0924 (base, bf16), 5k x 512-token
WT103 chunks, fused-backward SGD lr 5e-4, RTX 3090. ~70 GPU-min.
Results: `results/results_moe/`.

## Engineering result first: a 7B MoE continuous learner in 15.3GB VRAM

At 512 tokens x top-8, nearly every expert is touched per chunk, so conventional
backward-then-step would hold ~13GB of expert grads next to 14GB of weights. Fix:
**fused-backward SGD** — every trainable parameter carries a post-accumulate-grad
hook that clips, applies its update, and frees the gradient the moment autograd
produces it. Peak grad memory ≈ one layer; measured peak 15.25GB (experts) /
15.48GB (full). The model updates WHILE the error signal propagates through it.
~300ms/chunk. This is the pattern that makes consumer-hardware continuous learning
of large sparse models practical, and it composes with Colibrì's expert streaming.

## Findings

1. **The MoE continuous learner works.** Real domain gains (−0.14..−0.18 held-out CE,
   +0.14..+0.18 prequential edge) against small off-domain forgetting (+0.03..+0.04
   nats on TinyStories) after 5k updates. At this dose, a pretrained MoE keeps its
   learnings while genuinely learning the stream — Peter's question answered
   affirmatively for the afternoon-scale regime.
2. **The pre-registered trunk-interference prediction FAILED — inverted.** Full-model
   adaptation beats experts-only on BOTH axes: faster stream adaptation (+0.179 vs
   +0.135) AND less off-domain forgetting (+0.026 vs +0.043). Mechanism hypothesis
   (stated as such): **concentrating updates concentrates the damage** — freezing the
   trunk forces all adaptation pressure through expert weights, distorting them more
   per-weight, and the off-domain behaviors routed through those same experts pay for
   it; full-model adaptation spreads the change thinly (and lets the router adjust).
   The round-4 intuition (shard the substrate to contain interference) holds for
   *capacity* but not for *update pressure* at this dose.
3. **Dose caveat, prominently**: 5k updates. Experts-only has a property no
   measurement at this horizon can price: the trunk stays pristine FOREVER, which
   may dominate at 10^5–10^6 updates where distributed trunk erosion compounds.
   The long-horizon crossover, if any, is the follow-up that matters.
4. **Router-skew cross-validation**: the base model's routing concentration
   (top-26% of experts carry 42.7% of routes) closely matches Colibrì's independent
   measurement on the instruct variant (47.6%) — two instruments, two variants,
   same structure.
5. **Instrument bug, honestly logged**: per-expert update-mass telemetry came back
   all-zero — transformers 5.x stores OLMoE experts as STACKED tensors
   (`experts.gate_up_proj: (64, 2048, 2048)`), so name-based expert attribution
   never fired. Training was unaffected (zero-grad rows get zero updates — sparsity
   held). Fix for next round: per-expert row-norms computed inside the hook. The
   expert-localization question ("living library") is deferred one round.

## Program state

Rounds 4 and 8 now bracket the architecture question beautifully: at toy scale,
routing-contained adaptation beat frozen specialists; at 7B, *unconstrained*
adaptation beats routing-contained adaptation at short horizons. The synthesis
candidate: containment is the right *long-horizon* policy and distributed change
the right *short-horizon* one — which is testable (long-run arm) and, if true, means
the gate/containment machinery should engage progressively, not statically.
